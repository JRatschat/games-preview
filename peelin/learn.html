Learn Peelin

Peelin is a quick card game for two. You race to get rid of your cards, but you may never sort them. This page teaches it one idea at a time, as simply as possible.

The whole game in short

  1. You each hold 11 cards in a row, and you may never change their order.
  2. You take turns playing cards that beat the last play, and the first player to run out of cards wins the deal.
  3. You start from the two ends of your row, but you may beat with cards from anywhere in it. Queens are the strongest cards, but they can only be played from an end.
  4. Before you play, you each secretly bet one card. Whoever runs out first scores points for the cards left in the other player’s hand and for both bets.
Your row of cards. You start from the ends.23Q50413

What you need, and how this page works

  • Two players.
  • A Trifle deck of 28 cards, or 28 cards from a standard deck (see below).
  • Pen and paper to keep score.

From a standard deck, take all four Jacks, Aces, 2s, 3s, 4s, 5s and Queens. The Jacks count as 0 and the Aces as 1.

Part 1 Playing cards

0The cards

The deck has 28 cards, four each of 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and the Queen. Here they are from lowest to highest.

012345Q

The Queen is the highest card. The cards also show suits or colours, but in Peelin they don’t matter. Two 4s are just two 4s, so this page draws the cards without them.

The word A card’s number, or Queen, is its rank.

Check yourself. Which is higher, a 5 or a Queen? And a 0 or a 1?

The Queen is higher than the 5. The 1 is higher than the 0.

1Your cards stay in order

Shuffle and deal 12 cards to each player. Put the last 4 cards face down to one side. Nobody uses them.

Pick up your 12 cards as a stack and fan them out without changing their order. Many card games let you sort your cards. Not this one. Your cards stay in the order you were dealt them for the whole deal.

Your hand, left to right40Q25133Q025

The cards at the two ends of your hand matter most. You’ll see why in step 3.

The word The leftmost and the rightmost card are on the sides of your hand.

Check yourself. Your hand is 4 0 Q 2 5 1. Which cards are on the sides?
Left side and right side40Q251

The 4 on the left side and the 1 on the right side.

2What you can play

You play one card or two cards at a time. There are three kinds.

A single is any one card

3

A pair is two cards of the same rank, next to each other

44

A run is two cards with ranks in a row, next to each other

34

A run can go either way, so 3‑4 and 4‑3 are both runs. Queens are never part of a run, so the highest run is 4‑5.

The two cards of a pair or run must sit next to each other in your hand. You can’t pick two cards from different places.

The words A single, a pair or a run is a combination. Cards next to each other are adjacent.

Check yourself. Your hand is 3 4 4 Q 1 0. Which pairs and runs are in it?
Your hand344Q10

The pair 4‑4, the run 3‑4 (with the first 4) and the run 1‑0. 4‑Q and Q‑1 are not runs, because Queens are never part of a run.

3Starting from a side

The dealer’s opponent starts (who deals is in Part 3). They play any combination, but only from a side of their hand.

  • a single must be the leftmost or the rightmost card,
  • a pair or run must be the two leftmost or the two rightmost cards.
You may start with 2, 0, the run 2‑3 or the pair 0‑023Q500

You can’t start with the Queen or the 5 here, because they are not on a side.

The words Playing the first combination is leading. Everything played until someone passes (step 5) is a trick.

Check yourself. Your hand is Q 4 1 2 5 5. What may you lead?

The single Queen (it’s on the left side), the single 5 and the pair 5‑5 on the right side. Q‑4 is not a combination.

4Beating from anywhere

Now the other player answers. To beat the last play, play the same kind of combination, equal or higher.

  • a single beats a single, a pair beats a pair, a run beats a run,
  • an equal combination beats too, so a 3 beats a 3,
  • a run counts as its higher card, so 4‑5 beats 3‑4, and 4‑3 beats 3‑4.

When you beat, you may play any single card, or any two adjacent cards, from anywhere in your hand.

Queens are the exception. A Queen can only ever be played from a side, also when beating. Two Queens as a pair must be the two leftmost or the two rightmost cards.

Your opponent led a single 3. You may beat with the 3 or the 5.23Q500

The 2 and the 0s are lower than 3. The Queen is higher, but it isn’t on a side.

The gap closes

When a card leaves the middle of your hand, the cards on both sides of the gap become adjacent.

You beat with the 523Q00
Now Q and 0 are adjacent23Q00

Then it’s the first player’s turn again. They may beat your 5, and so on, back and forth.

Check yourself. Your opponent leads the pair 1‑1. Your hand is 0 0 3 4 4 Q. What can beat it?

Only the pair 4‑4. It’s in the middle, but beating may come from anywhere. 0‑0 is lower, and 3‑4 is a run, not a pair.

Your hand2Q5[0]
Check yourself. Your opponent leads a single 5. Which of your cards can beat it?

Only the 5, because an equal card beats. The Queen is higher, but it isn’t on a side, so you can’t play it.

5Passing, and going out

On your turn, instead of beating, you may pass. You may pass even if you could beat. Only the player who leads may not pass.

When a player passes, the trick ends.

  1. All its cards go face down on the discard pile. Nobody may look through it.
  2. The player who played last leads the next trick, again from a side.

When you play your last card, you go out, and the deal ends at once.

Check yourself. You lead a 2. Your opponent beats it with a 4. You beat that with a 5. Your opponent passes. Who leads next?

You, because you played last.

Try it now. Play one practice deal with steps 0 to 5. Deal 12 cards each and set 4 aside. Skip the bet and don’t pick up cards. The first player to go out wins.

Part 2 Picking, betting and scoring

6Picking a card when you pass

When you pass, you may take one card from the combination your opponent just played. Put it anywhere into your hand, between two cards or on a side. The rest of the trick goes on the discard pile as usual.

This is the only way to put a card where you want it. A good pick can complete a pair or run, or put a useful card on a side.

You lead the pair 0‑023Q500
Your opponent beats it with 4‑444
You pass and pick a 4. You put it between the Queen and the 5.23Q45

Now the run 4‑5 is on your right side, ready to lead.

The word Taking a card when you pass is picking.

Picking is up to you. Every card you pick is one more card you have to play before you go out.

Check yourself. Your hand is 0 Q 3. You pass on your opponent’s pair 2‑2. Where could you put a 2 to have a run on a side?

Either after the 3, 0 Q 3 2, or between the Queen and the 3, 0 Q 2 3. Both put a run on your right side. Next to the 0 it would be 2‑0, which is not a run.

7The secret bet

Right after the deal, before anyone plays, both players choose one card from anywhere in their hand. Both place it face down in front of them at the same time.

You take out the 5 …40Q25133Q025
… and bet it, face down

Now you hold 11 cards, and the gap closes as usual. Your bet is out of your hand. You can’t play it, but you may look at it at any time. Any card may be your bet, even a Queen.

The word Your bet is your stake.

Whoever goes out wins both stakes (step 8). So a Queen is a big bet on yourself, but it leaves your hand weaker. A 0 is a safe bet, and it takes a weak card out of your hand.

Check yourself. Your opponent goes out. Who gets the points for your stake?

Your opponent. Whoever goes out scores both stakes.

8Points, and winning the match

When a player goes out, both players turn their stakes face up. Each card is worth points.

The player who went out scores the points of every card left in the other player’s hand, plus both stakes. The other player scores nothing.

You went out, so you score 10 + 3 + 0 + 4 + 2 = 19.

Add up your points over the deals. The first player to reach 100 points wins the match. For a short match, play to 50.

Check yourself. You go out. Your opponent still holds a 5 and a 0, and staked a Queen. You staked a 1. How many points do you score?

5 + 0 + 10 + 1 = 16.

Part 3 More details

Small rules

Who deals, and who leads first

Choose the dealer for the first deal at random. After that, the player with the higher score deals, so the player who is behind leads. If the scores are tied, the player who went out in the last deal deals.

The dealer’s opponent leads the first trick of each deal.

The 4 unused cards

The 4 cards set aside after the deal stay face down. Nobody uses them or looks at them during the deal.

A match of a set length

Instead of playing to 100 or 50 points, you may agree on any number of deals or points. With a number of deals, the higher score wins. A tie goes to the player who went out in the last deal.

Playing with other decks

With a numbered deck such as Sticheln, take the 0 to 5 and the 10 in four colours. The 10 counts as the Queen.

Keeping score with chips

Instead of pen and paper, you can use poker chips as counters.

Tips for your first matches

  • Look at your sides before each lead. They decide what you can start with.
  • Cards in the middle are your reserve for beating, since beating may come from anywhere.
  • A Queen in the middle can’t be played. Play the cards on one side of it to bring it to a side.
  • You may pass even when you could beat. Saving a strong card can be worth more than winning a trick.
  • Before you pick, think about where the card would go. A card that completes a pair or run on a side is worth taking.
  • Stake a weak card that is in your way, or a Queen when your hand looks strong.
