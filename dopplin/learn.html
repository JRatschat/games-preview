Learn Dopplin

Dopplin is a card game for two. You race to play out all your cards, and before you start you may promise to win under harder conditions for more points. This page teaches it one idea at a time, as simply as possible.

The whole game in short

  1. You each get 18 cards. You take turns playing them, and each play must beat the one before.
  2. When a player can’t or won’t beat, the other player starts again with anything they like. The first player to play out all their cards wins.
  3. Aces are the weakest cards, but they can steal your opponent’s pair or set into your own hand.
  4. Before you play, you may bid a contract, a harder way to win that is worth more points. The first player to 150 points wins the match.

What you need, and how this page works

  • Two players.
  • Two standard decks with Jokers.
  • Pen and paper, or poker chips, to keep score.

From both decks, keep only the Aces, 2s, 3s, 4s, 5s, Jacks, Queens and Jokers, and then take out all the Diamonds. That leaves 46 cards, 6 of each rank and 4 Jokers.

You can also play Dopplin online against bots or friends at play.cards.

Part 1 (steps 0 to 6) is how cards are played. After step 6 you play a practice hand. Part 2 (steps 7 to 10) adds bidding a contract and the scoring. Part 3 holds smaller rules. They matter in play, but you don’t need them while learning. If you forget what a word means, Part 3 also has a list of words.

Already know climbing games such as Big Two? Read step 0 for the card order and steps 2 and 3 for the combinations, since they are Dopplin’s own. Then go on to step 6.

Part 1 Playing cards

0The cards

Suits don’t matter in Dopplin, with one exception. The 2 Queens of Hearts are the strongest cards of all. The other 4 Queens are called ordinary Queens. There are 2 Small Jokers and 2 Big Jokers. Here are all the ranks, from lowest to highest.

A2345JQSJBJQ♥︎

So the Ace is the lowest card here, and Jokers are not wild. A Joker is just a very high card.

If your Jokers don’t show which is Small and which is Big, agree on it before you play.

Check yourself. Which is higher, a Jack or a 5? A Big Joker or an ordinary Queen? A Heart Queen or a Big Joker?

The Jack beats the 5. The Big Joker beats the ordinary Queen. The Heart Queen beats everything, even the Big Joker.

1Dealing

Shuffle and deal 18 cards to each player. Put the other 10 cards face down to one side. They are only used by some contracts in Part 2.

Sort your cards in any way you like. Most players sort them from low to high.

The word Your cards are your hand. One round of play, from dealing until someone is out of cards, is also called a hand.

2Singles, pairs and sets

On your turn you play one card or several cards together. These are the simple kinds.

A single, any 1 card4
A pair, 2 of a rankJJ
A set, 3 to 6 of a rank333

There are three rules for the special cards.

  • The two Heart Queens may pair with each other, but never with an ordinary Queen.
  • Jokers only go as singles or as a pair of the same kind, so two Small Jokers or two Big Jokers.
  • Jokers never stand in for other cards.

The word Each way of playing cards together is a combination.

The low cards, A to 5, can also be played in a row.

4Leading and beating

The player who starts plays any combination they like. That’s leading. The other player may then beat it, pass, or use Aces to steal it (step 6).

To beat a play, you play the same kind of combination with the same number of cards, and it must be strictly higher.

  • An equal play does not beat. A 3 doesn’t beat a 3.
  • A pair only beats a pair, and a set of three only beats a set of three.
  • Runs and connected sets compare by their highest card. Connected sets must have the same number of groups and the same group size.
  • Nothing beats a different kind of play. Four of a kind doesn’t beat a pair.
Your opponent leads33
You could beat it with any higher pair, for example55

A 3‑3 doesn’t beat it, because it’s equal. A single 5 or a set of 4‑4‑4 doesn’t beat it either, because they are the wrong kind.

After you beat, it’s your opponent’s turn again. They may beat your play, and so on, back and forth.

Check yourself. Your opponent leads the run 2‑3‑4‑5. Does A‑2‑3‑4‑5 beat it? Does A‑2‑3‑4?

No to both. A‑2‑3‑4‑5 has 5 cards, not 4. A‑2‑3‑4 has the right length, but its highest card is 4, which is lower than 5.

5Passing, and playing out

Instead of beating, you may pass. You may pass even if you could beat. The player who leads may not pass.

When a player passes, the other player, who played last, clears the pile. The played cards are put aside, and that player leads again with anything they like.

When you play your last card, you shed out, and the hand ends at once. Your opponent can’t answer your final play.

Check yourself. You lead a 5. Your opponent beats it with a Jack. You pass. What happens next?

Your opponent played last, so they clear the pile and lead again.

6Aces steal with the shovel

Aces are the lowest cards, but they have a special power. When your opponent has just played a pair or set, you may play Aces to take it. You need one Ace fewer than the cards you take.

You playYou take
Aa pair
AAa set of 3
AAAa set of 4
AAAAa set of 5
AAAAAa set of 6

The stolen cards go into your hand. Your Aces stay on the pile as the new play to beat. Your opponent may beat them, steal them with Aces of their own, or pass.

Your opponent playsQQQ
You playAA
You take into your handQQQ

Now A‑A is the play to beat, so any higher pair beats it.

You can’t steal Heart Queens, Jokers, singles, runs or connected sets. You can only steal the play your opponent just made.

The word Stealing with Aces is the shovel. You shovel a pair or set.

Check yourself. Your opponent plays four Jacks. How many Aces do you need to shovel them? Could you shovel a pair of Big Jokers?

3 Aces, one fewer than the 4 Jacks. And no, Jokers and Heart Queens can never be shoveled.

Try it now. Play one practice hand with steps 0 to 6. Deal 18 cards each and set 10 aside. Skip the bidding and the scoring. Choose at random who leads first. The first player to shed out wins.

Part 2 Contracts and scoring

7Bidding a contract

Before the first card is played, you may promise to win in a harder way, for more points. That promise is a contract. There are six, from weakest to strongest.

Breakaway→Forced Shovel→Buried Treasure→Red Finish→The Chase→Shutout

The bidding goes back and forth, starting with the first bidder. On your chance, you either pass or declare a contract stronger than the last one.

  • When a player passes after a contract has been declared, the bidding ends. The player who declared last plays that contract.
  • If both players pass before anyone declares, there is no contract. You play the hand like your practice hand. This is called Quiet.
  • Declaring Shutout ends the bidding at once, since nothing is stronger.

In every contract, you must shed out before your opponent. Unless the contract says otherwise, you lead first. If your opponent sheds out first, you fail.

The words The player who plays the contract is the declarer. The other player is the non-declarer.

Check yourself. You bid first and pass. Your opponent declares Breakaway. You declare Buried Treasure. Your opponent passes. Who plays which contract?

You play Buried Treasure. Your first pass didn’t end the bidding, because no contract had been declared yet. Your opponent’s pass after your declaration did.

8Three contracts to start with

The rules suggest starting with these three. Quiet still applies whenever both players pass.

Breakaway
Nothing extra. Lead first and shed out before your opponent.
Buried Treasure
Before play, turn up the top 3 of the 10 set-aside cards. Your opponent takes 1 or 2 of them into their hand, and you take the rest.
Red Finish
Your opponent leads first. Your final play must contain a Heart Queen.
Check yourself. You declared Red Finish and hold both Heart Queens. Why might you keep them until the end?

Your final play must contain a Heart Queen. If you play both early, you can’t finish the contract.

Try it now. Play a few hands with only these three contracts before adding the others.

9The other three contracts

Forced Shovel
Until your final play, you may play Aces only to shovel, never in a single, pair, set, run or connected set. Whenever you can shovel, you must. Your final play may contain Aces in any way.
The Chase
Before play, you take the top 3 set-aside cards into your hand. Your opponent leads first. Your opponent escapes as soon as they have fewer than 3 cards after playing on their turn, and then you fail. So you must shed out before that. A shovel can’t make them escape.
Shutout
You must shed out before your opponent plays any card. If they play a card first, even to shovel, you fail at once.
Check yourself. In The Chase, your opponent holds 4 cards and plays a pair. What happens?

They now hold 2 cards, which is fewer than 3. They escape, and you fail.

Your handA5QQ
Check yourself. You declared Forced Shovel. Your opponent plays a pair of Jacks. What may you do?

You must shovel the Jacks with your Ace. Without the contract, you could also beat with Q‑Q or pass. In Forced Shovel, you must shovel whenever you can.

10Points, and winning the match

Each contract has two values. If the declarer succeeds, they score the first. If they fail, their opponent scores the second.

ContractSuccessFailure
Quiet9–
Breakaway1510
Forced Shovel2114
Buried Treasure2718
Red Finish4228

In Quiet, whoever sheds out first scores 9.

The player who scores also gets a card bonus of 1 point for each card left in their opponent’s hand. The bonus is never more than the score from the table, and there is no bonus for Shutout.

The first player to reach 150 points wins the match. For a longer match, play to 250.

Check yourself. You declare Breakaway and fail. You still hold 12 cards. How many points does your opponent score?

10 for your failed Breakaway, plus a card bonus of 12, capped at 10. That makes 20.

Part 3 More details

Small rules

Who bids first

Choose the first bidder at random for the first hand. After that, the player with fewer points bids first. If the scores are tied, the player who didn’t score in the last hand bids first.

In Quiet, the first bidder leads.

The 10 set-aside cards

They stay face down and unused. Only Buried Treasure and The Chase take cards from the top of them.

When a hand ends early

A hand usually ends when someone sheds out. It can also end earlier, when the declarer fails a contract during play, as in Shutout, or when the non-declarer escapes in The Chase.

A match of a set length

Instead of 150 or 250 points, you may agree on any number of hands or points. A points match ends after the hand in which someone reaches the target.

Keeping score with chips

Poker chips make scoring easy, but pen and paper work too.

Tips for your first matches

  • Don’t spend your Aces too early. The shovel is often your best way back into a hand.
  • Watch how many cards your opponent holds. Every card left is a bonus point for whoever scores.
  • Before you bid, think about how you’ll play your last few cards. Many contracts are won or lost at the end.
  • Contracts that use the set-aside cards are a gamble. The new cards may help you, or change your hand in ways you didn’t expect.
