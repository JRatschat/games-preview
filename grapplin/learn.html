Learn Grapplin

Grapplin is a card game for two. You race to get rid of your cards, and you can grab your opponent’s play and twist it before you beat it. This page teaches it one idea at a time, as simply as possible.

The whole game in short

  1. You each get 18 cards. You take turns playing them, and each play must beat the one before.
  2. When a player passes, the other player starts again with anything they like. The first player out of cards wins the round.
  3. If you hold a card of the same rank as one in your opponent’s play, you may show it to change their play, or to take new cards, before you beat it.
  4. Once per round, you may flip your Joker to turn the order of the cards upside down. The first player to win 3 rounds wins the game.

What you need, and how this page works

  • Two players.
  • One standard deck of 52 cards and its two Jokers.

You can also play Grapplin online on Virtual Tabletop.

Part 1 (steps 0 to 4) is how cards are played. After step 4 you play a practice round. Part 2 (steps 5 to 9) adds matching a card and the Joker, which make Grapplin special. Part 3 holds smaller rules and a list of words.

Already know climbing games such as Big Two? Look at steps 1 and 2, then go on to step 5.

Part 1 Playing cards

0The cards

A deck has 52 cards in 4 suits, two red and two black.

  • ♥︎ Hearts, red
  • ♦︎ Diamonds, red
  • ♠︎ Spades, black
  • ♣︎ Clubs, black

Each suit has 13 cards. Here they are from lowest to highest.

2345678910JQKA

The 2 is the lowest card, and the Ace the highest. Suits only matter for one special play, in step 2.

The two Jokers are not played. Each player gets one face up in front of them (step 9).

The word A card’s number or letter is its rank.

Check yourself. Which of these are combinations? 7‑8, Q‑K‑A, 5‑6‑7‑8‑9, 4‑4.

7‑8 is a run of 2, Q‑K‑A a run of 3 and 4‑4 a pair. 5‑6‑7‑8‑9 is not, because a run has at most 4 cards.

2Bombs

Two special plays are stronger than any combination.

Four of a rank5♠︎5♥︎5♦︎5♣︎
A run of 4 in one suit7♣︎8♣︎9♣︎10♣︎

Four of a rank is always stronger than a run of 4 in one suit.

The word These are bombs. Four of a rank is called quads.

Check yourself. Does ♣︎7 ♣︎8 ♣︎9 ♣︎10 beat 5‑5‑5‑5?

No. Quads are always stronger than a run of 4 in one suit, whatever the ranks.

3Leading and beating

The dealer’s opponent starts the round with any combination or bomb. That’s leading.

To beat it, the other player plays the same kind of combination with the same number of cards, but higher. A pair beats a pair, and a run of 3 beats a run of 3. A bomb beats any combination, and a higher bomb beats a bomb. Then it’s the first player’s turn again, and so on, back and forth.

Your opponent leads9♦︎10♣︎J♠︎
You beat it with10♥︎J♦︎Q♠︎
Check yourself. Your opponent leads 9‑10‑J. Can you beat it with 10‑J‑Q? With Q‑Q? With 4‑4‑4‑4?

10‑J‑Q yes, it is a higher run of 3. Q‑Q no, it is a pair, not a run. 4‑4‑4‑4 yes, because a bomb beats any combination.

4Passing, and winning

Instead of beating, you may pass. Then all played cards go on a discard pile, and your opponent leads again with anything they like.

The first player to play out all their cards wins the round and scores 1 point. The first player to reach 3 points wins the game. After each round, the other player deals.

Check yourself. You lead 6‑6. Your opponent beats it with K‑K, and you pass. Who leads next?

Your opponent, because you passed.

Try it now. Play one practice round with steps 0 to 4. Deal 18 cards to each player and set the rest aside. Leave out the Jokers. Only beat or pass. The first player out of cards wins.

Part 2 Matching and the Joker

5Matching a card

When your opponent has played a combination, look for a card in your hand with the same rank as one of theirs. The suit doesn’t matter. Show it, and you may do one special action (steps 6 to 8). Then you must beat their play.

  • You may match only one card per turn.
  • You can’t match a card in a bomb.
  • If you couldn’t beat the play afterwards, you may not match at all.

Your matched card always leaves your hand. So adding or removing a card also brings you closer to winning. Taking cards from the row doesn’t, but it can get you the cards you need.

The word Showing a card of the same rank is matching.

Check yourself. Your opponent plays 7‑7. You hold a 7 but no pair higher than 7s and no bomb. May you match?

Yes, if a special action lets you beat afterwards. For example, remove one 7, so their pair becomes a single 7, and beat it with any higher single. If no special action would let you beat, you may not match.

6Take cards from the row

At the start of each round, after dealing, put the 16 other cards face down as a draw pile. Turn its top 3 cards face up in a row next to it.

Draw pile
The row8♦︎K♠︎3♥︎

As a special action, discard your matched card and take 1 or 2 cards from the row into your hand. Then beat the play. After that, discard the rightmost card of the row, slide the others to the right, and refill the row to 3 cards from the draw pile while it has cards.

You can only take cards while at least one card is in the row.

7Add a card to their play

As a special action, add one card from your hand to your opponent’s combination, so that it becomes a different combination or a bomb. Then you must beat the new play.

You add your matched 66♠︎6♥︎6♦︎
Now you beat the triple8♠︎8♥︎8♣︎

You may add your matched card or any other card. If you add another card, discard your matched card.

8Remove a card from their play

As a special action, remove one card from your opponent’s combination, so that it becomes a different combination. Discard your matched card, then beat the new play.

Your opponent plays3♣︎4♦︎5♥︎
Without the 5, it’s a run of 23♣︎4♦︎
You beat it7♠︎8♥︎

If your opponent led a single, you can remove it entirely. Discard your matched card, and then you lead any combination or bomb yourself.

Check yourself. Your opponent leads a single King, and you hold a King. What can you do?

Match it, remove their King and discard yours. Then you lead anything you like, and it’s their turn to answer.

9Flip your Joker

At the start of each round, each player puts one Joker face up in front of them. When you answer your opponent’s play, you may turn your Joker face down. The order of the cards turns upside down, so the 2 is now the highest card and the Ace the lowest.

AKQJ1098765432

After flipping, you may still match a card. Then you must beat the play in the new order. If you couldn’t, you may not flip.

  • Your opponent can’t flip their Joker to turn the order back until someone has passed and a new play has been led.
  • Once both Jokers are face down, the usual order is back, and nobody can flip any more this round.
Check yourself. Your Joker is face down and your opponent’s is face up. Which is higher, a 2 or an Ace?

The 2. With one Joker face down, the order is upside down.

Part 3 More details

Small rules

Who deals and who starts

If you can’t decide, choose the first dealer at random. The dealer’s opponent leads first. After each round, the other player deals.

Setting up a round

Put one Joker face up in front of each player. Shuffle the other 52 cards and deal 18 to each player. The other 16 form the draw pile, and its top 3 cards are turned face up as the row.

Tips for your first games

  • Adding or removing a card also gets rid of your matched card, so look for a match every turn.
  • Keep an eye on the row. A good card there can be worth a match.
  • A bomb takes back control, so save it for when you need it.
  • Save your Joker for when the order works against you.
