Jugglin is a standard-deck Tarock game for three or four, where every tarock has a twin and the Pagat beats the Fool.

3 players · 45–60 min · teach in 15 min · version 3.2, updated 8 Nov 2026 · 2026

You need One standard deck with Jokers

Rules PDF with player reference and game board: 3 players, 4 players · PlayingCards.io file

Differences

Both versions use the same deck, card ranking, Juggler’s Trick, bids and moments.

In 3p, the Declarer always plays alone against the other two. Besides the Point game, there’s Tightrope, where the Declarer may win at most one trick.

In 4p, the Declarer calls a King, and whoever holds it becomes their secret partner. Nobody knows the sides until that King hits the table. Once the partners are known, both sides exchange a card. If you call your own King, you play alone against the other three.

Ramsch also plays differently. In 3p, the player with the most card points pays 3 chips to each of the others, so you just need to avoid taking the most. In 4p, each player pays 2 chips to every player with fewer card points.

Designer’s note

Wow. This game makes you feel things, good and bad. It has a serious core, but then there’s also lots of laughter and Schadenfreude. The 3p version is a bit more strategic, the 4p version a bit more exciting IMO. Obviously, this Tarock game is played with a regular deck of playing cards. Through this, there are two copies of each tarock. Why be happy with one Fool when you could have two Fools? Unfortunately, the Pagats (the lowest tarocks) are now able to beat them. And if it happens, it hurts no matter if you’ll win the contract or not. Ouch.

Concept

Jugglin is a point-trick game for three with a standard deck of 52 cards and its two Jokers. The Jokers, the Aces, and the 2s to 5s are the tarocks, which are permanent trumps.

Players bid on how many talon cards they want. The fewer cards, the higher the bid. The auction winner becomes the Declarer and plays alone against the other two players, the Defenders. In a Point game, the Declarer needs 41 of the 80 card points. In Tightrope, they may win at most one trick. If nobody bids, everyone plays Ramsch alone.

The deck holds two Fools, two Monds, and two Pagats, the Honours of the game. A Pagat, normally the lowest tarock, beats a Fool whenever they meet in a trick. This is the Juggler’s Trick.

Every deal is played for chips. In a Point game, certain moments also pay immediately to the winner of a trick, such as a King meeting its Queen or one Honour capturing another. After three rounds, the player with the most chips wins.

Setup

Players: 3.

Cards: A standard deck of 52 cards and its two Jokers, 54 cards in all.

Chips: All payments in these rules are counted in chips. The best way to play is with poker chips or tokens worth 1 and 5, about 200 chips’ worth in total. Each player starts with 50 chips. All other chips stay in the box as the bank. Without poker chips, keep score on paper (see Keeping Score).

Board: Optional. It lies in the middle of the table as a play aid. A coin on it marks the contract.

Direction: Dealing, bidding, and play go clockwise.

Dealer: Choose the first dealer at random. After each deal, the player to the dealer’s left deals next.

Forehand: The player to the dealer’s left. Forehand bids first and leads the first trick, except in Tightrope.

Match: A match is 3 rounds. In each round, every player deals once, so a match has 9 deals.

A Deal at a Glance

Each deal follows these steps. The sections below explain each one.

  1. Deal: 6 cards face down as the talon, then 16 cards to each player.

  2. Auction: Forehand first. Bid higher (Three, Two, One, Zero) or pass for good. If all pass, play Ramsch.

  3. Talon: In Three, Two, or One, turn it face up. The Declarer takes a group and discards as many cards, tarocks face up. In Zero, it goes unseen to the Defenders. In Ramsch, it stays face down.

  4. Game type: Point game, or after a bid of One or Zero also Tightrope (at most one trick).

  5. Tricks: Forehand leads, or the Declarer in Tightrope. In a Point game, pay the moments after each trick.

  6. Settling: The Declarer settles the game with each Defender. In Ramsch, the player with the most card points pays.

Cards

Tarocks: The 22 tarocks are permanent trumps. From high to low, they rank:

  1. The two Jokers, the Fools.

  2. The two black Aces, the Monds.

  3. The 5s, 4s, 3s, and 2s, by number. Between two cards of the same number, the black card beats the red one. For example, a red 5 beats a black 4, and a black 4 beats a red 4.

  4. The two red Aces, the Pagats.

The Fools, the Monds, and the Pagats are the Honours.

Suits: Each suit has eight suit cards. From high to low, they rank King, Queen, Jack, 10, 9, 8, 7, 6. An Ace or a card from 2 to 5 is always a tarock, never a suit card.

Twins: Two tarocks of the same number and colour are twins, for example the two black Aces, or the 5 of clubs and the 5 of spades. The two Jokers are twins as well. Between twins in the same trick, the one played first ranks higher.

The Juggler’s Trick: When a Fool and a Pagat are in the same trick, the Pagat wins it, whatever else is in the trick. With twin Pagats, the one played first wins. This is the only exception to the ranking.

Honour cycle: When an Honour wins a trick, it captures every other Honour in that trick. For example, the Pagat captures the Fool, the Fool captures the Mond, and the Mond captures the Pagat. A twin can also capture its twin. In a Point game, each capture pays (see Moments).

Card points: Table 1 lists the card points. Each trick won is worth 1 card point. Talon cards and discards count only the card points of their cards, never as a trick. A complete deal has 80 card points: 64 on the cards and 16 from the tricks.

Table 1. Card points
Card Card points
Honours: Fools, Monds, Pagats 4
King 4
Queen 3
Jack 2
10 1
Any other card 0
Each trick won 1
Total 80

Deal

The dealer shuffles, and the player to the dealer’s right cuts. The dealer places the top 6 cards face down as the talon. Then the dealer deals packets of 8 cards, starting with Forehand, until each player has 16 cards.

Before the auction, a player who has no tarocks shows their hand, and the same dealer deals again.

Auction

Forehand speaks first, then the auction goes clockwise. On your turn, either bid higher than the current bid, or pass. The first bid can be any bid. Once you pass, you are out of the auction for this deal.

There are four bids, listed from lowest to highest in Table 2. The number tells how many talon cards the Declarer takes.

Table 2. Bids
Bid Talon cards Bid value
Three 3 2
Two 2 4
One 1 6
Zero None 10

The auction ends when two players have passed and the third has bid. That player becomes the Declarer with their last bid. If all three players pass, play Ramsch.

Example: A is Forehand. A bids Three, B bids Two, and C passes. A bids One, and B passes. A is the Declarer with One.

Talon

In Three, Two, and One, turn the six talon cards face up without changing their order. They form consecutive groups: two groups of 3 in Three, three groups of 2 in Two, or six single cards in One.

The Declarer takes one group into their hand and then discards the same number of cards onto their trick pile. Kings and Honours may not be discarded. Discarded tarocks are placed face up. Other discards go face down. The remaining talon cards go face down onto the Defenders’ trick pile.

In Zero, the talon stays face down and goes straight to the Defenders’ trick pile without being seen by anyone.

In Ramsch, the talon stays face down in the middle of the table until the winners of the first six tricks take its cards as gifts (see Ramsch).

Game Types

After the talon, the Declarer names one of two game types, listed in Table 3.

Table 3. Game types
Game type The Declarer must Game value
Point game Take at least 41 card points. Bid value + margin
Tightrope Win at most one trick. Only after a bid of One or Zero. Bid value + 2

Tightrope: The Declarer leads the first trick. As soon as they win a second trick, the game is lost and the deal ends. The negative rules apply (see Trick Play).

Trick Play

Following: You must play a card of the led suit if you can. If you cannot, you must play a tarock if you can. If you can do neither, play any card. When a tarock is led, you must play a tarock if you can, and doing so counts as following suit.

Winning: The highest tarock wins the trick, but if the trick contains a Fool and a Pagat, the Pagat wins. If the trick has no tarock, the highest card of the led suit wins. The winner leads the next trick.

Point game: There is no duty to beat the cards already played.

Negative rules: In Tightrope and Ramsch, two more rules apply to all players. A card beats the trick so far when it would win the trick if no more cards were played. A twin never beats its twin. Your legal cards are the cards that Following allows.

  1. If one of your legal cards beats the trick so far, you must play such a card.

  2. You may only play a Pagat when a Fool is in the trick and no Pagat is in it yet, or when all your legal cards are Pagats. This also applies when you lead.

Rule 2 keeps the Pagats back for catching a Fool. They cannot be used as a cheap trump or a safe low tarock.

Trick piles: The Declarer keeps one trick pile, and the Defenders share one. In Ramsch, everyone keeps their own.

Moments

Moments apply only in Point games, never in Tightrope or Ramsch (Table 4).

Opponents: Moments only pay between opponents, which are the Declarer and each Defender. Defenders never pay each other.

Who pays: The trick’s winner collects every payment.

Calling: Before taking the trick, its winner calls out the moments in it, and the payers pay at once. Until the next card is led, or after the last trick until counting begins, any player may still call a missed moment.

Table 4. Moments
Moment In a trick Payment
Queen’s Ransom A King and the Queen of its suit. The Queen’s owner pays 1.
Fallen King A led King, won by another player. Its owner pays 1.
Honour Capture An Honour captured by another Honour. Its owner pays 2.
Pagat Ultimo A Pagat in the 16th trick. If it wins, opponents pay 2 in total. If it loses, its owner pays 2.

Queen’s Ransom: A trick contains a King and the Queen of the same suit. The Queen’s owner pays the trick’s winner 1 chip as her ransom.

Fallen King: When you lead a King and another player wins the trick, you pay the trick’s winner 1 chip.

Honour Capture: When an Honour wins a trick, the owner of each other Honour in it pays the trick’s winner 2 chips.

Pagat Ultimo: When a Pagat wins the 16th trick, the winner’s opponents pay 2 chips in total. A Defender collects 2 chips from the Declarer. The Declarer collects 1 chip from each Defender.

When a Pagat loses the 16th trick, its owner pays the trick’s winner 2 chips, if they are opponents. A Pagat still in hand is always played to the 16th trick, so keeping a Pagat to the end is always an attempt. This combines with Honour Capture. If a Mond captures a Pagat in the 16th trick, the Pagat’s owner pays the winner 2 chips for each moment, 4 in total.

Example: Moments combine. B leads the King of hearts, C plays the Queen of hearts, and A, the Declarer, trumps with a black 3. B and C are Defenders. A collects 2 chips: B pays 1 for the Fallen King, and C pays 1 for the Queen’s Ransom.

Example: B leads a black 5, C plays a Fool, and A, the Declarer, plays the other Fool. C’s Fool was played first, so it wins and captures A’s Fool. A pays C 2 chips.

Counting

In a Point game, count the Declarer’s card points after the last trick: the card points of all cards in their trick pile, including their discards, plus 1 for each trick they won. The Defenders’ card points are 80 minus the Declarer’s total.

Tip: Count the smaller pile. Pull out the Honours, Kings, Queens, Jacks, and 10s, add up their card points, and add 1 for each trick. If it is the Defenders’ pile, subtract the total from 80.

Settling

Game value: Start with the bid value (Table 2). In Tightrope, add 2. In a Point game, add the margin from Table 5: find the Declarer’s card points in the row for a won or a lost game, and read the margin above.

Table 5. Margin
Margin 0 1 2 3 4
Won 41–46 47–52 53–58 59–64 65+
Lost 35–40 29–34 23–28 17–22 0–16

Payment: If the Declarer won the game, each Defender pays them the game value. If the Declarer lost, they pay the game value to each Defender.

Example: A plays Two as a Point game and takes 53 card points. The margin is 2, so the game value is 4 + 2 = 6. B and C each pay A 6 chips.

Ramsch

If all three players pass, everyone plays alone and tries to take as few card points as possible. The negative rules apply (see Trick Play).

Gifts: The winner of each of the first six tricks also takes the top card of the talon, shows it, and adds it to their trick pile. The gift has its normal card points but is not part of the trick.

Payment: Everyone counts their own card points, including 1 for each trick they won. The player with the most card points pays each other player 3 chips. If two players tie for the most, each of them pays the third player 3 chips.

Keeping Score

With poker chips: If you have to pay more chips than you have, borrow 10 chips from the bank, as often as you need. Note what you borrowed. If the bank runs out, note further loans on paper.

On paper: Write one column per player. Everyone starts at 0, and scores may go below 0. Each chip is one point on the score sheet. During the deal, note each moment payment, for example “A +1, B −1”. After the deal, add the game payment and write each player’s change for the deal in one row. Every row adds up to 0, which shows a mistake at once. Keep a running total for each player.

Match End

The match ends after 3 rounds. With poker chips, subtract any loans from your chip total. Totals may be below 0.

The player with the most chips wins. If players are tied, they share the win.

Design Notes and Credits

Jugglin is inspired by the Tarock games Slovenian Tarok, Königrufen, and Danish Grosstarock. I learned about them in Jerry Neill Furr’s Tarocchi, written under the name Philebus. Further inspiration came from Skat, Doppelkopf, and Sean Ross’s Beaver Tail.
