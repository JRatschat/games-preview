Learn Slamlin

Slamlin is a card game for two. One player promises a result and gives the other player advantages to make the promise worth more. This page teaches it one idea at a time, as simply as possible.

The whole game in short

  1. You play 10 tricks with 32 cards, and the cards you win are worth points.
  2. In the first 6 tricks you each play two cards, and the second ones go down face down at the same time, so you have to guess what the other player does.
  3. Before playing, you compete to become the Declarer by offering harder targets and advantages for your opponent.
  4. Both players score the card points they win. The contract is worth more, to the Declarer if they make it, or to their opponent if they fail, and then its levels count double.

What you need, and how this page works

  • Two players.
  • 32 cards from a standard deck, the 7s up to the Aces.
  • 10 tokens, which are 6 handicap tokens and a Schneider and a Schwarz for each player. The rules PDF has them as cards to print, and slips of paper work too.
  • A score sheet, or poker chips.

Part 1 (steps 0 to 5) is how cards are played. After step 5 you play a practice deal. Part 2 (steps 6 to 9) adds the contract the simple way, as in the first two deals of your first match. Part 3 (steps 10 to 14) adds Nullo, the handicaps and the auction. Part 4 holds smaller rules and other ways to play a match, and a list of words in case you forget one.

Already know Skat? Read steps 1, 2, 4 and 5, then go on to step 6.

Part 1 Playing cards

0The cards

Slamlin uses 32 cards, 8 in each of the 4 suits.

  • ♥︎ Hearts, red
  • ♦︎ Diamonds, red
  • ♠︎ Spades, black
  • ♣︎ Clubs, black

Each suit has the Ace, King, Queen, Jack, 10, 9, 8 and 7. All 32 cards are in play in every deal.

The word The symbol is the card’s suit.

1Trump, and the order of the cards

In most deals, one suit is trump. Any trump beats every card of the other suits. The player who chooses the game names it (step 7).

In the trump suit, the Jack and the 9 jump to the top.

The trump suit, highest to lowest (here hearts)J♥︎9♥︎A♥︎10♥︎K♥︎Q♥︎8♥︎7♥︎

In every other suit the order is the usual one, with the 10 just below the Ace.

Any other suit, highest to lowestA♠︎10♠︎K♠︎Q♠︎J♠︎9♠︎8♠︎7♠︎

There are also games without trump, No-trump and Nullo. Then every suit has the usual order.

Check yourself. Hearts are trump. Which is higher, 9♥︎ or A♥︎? A♠︎ or 7♥︎?

9♥︎, because the 9 is the second-highest trump. 7♥︎, because any trump beats every card of the other suits.

2Card points

At the end of a deal, the cards you won are worth points.

CardCard points
Jack of trump4
9 of trump3
Ace2

King1
Queen1

Winning the last trick2

So a trump game has 33 card points in all. Without trump, the Jacks and 9s are all worth 0, and there are 26.

Check yourself. Spades are trump. You win J♠︎, A♥︎, 9♦︎ and Q♣︎. How many card points is that?

4 for the Jack of trump, 2 for the Ace and 1 for the Queen. The 9♦︎ is not trump, so it is worth 0. That makes 7.

3Tricks, and following suit

A deal has 10 rounds of play. In each round, both players put cards on the table, and one of them wins them all. The cards of one round are a trick.

The first card of a trick decides its suit. Every other card in the trick must be of the same suit if you can. If you can’t, play any card, a trump or any other.

The highest trump in the trick wins. Without a trump, the highest card of the first card’s suit wins. The winner puts the trick face down on their own pile and starts the next one. Nobody may look at won tricks before scoring.

The words Playing the first card is leading, and its suit is the led suit.

Check yourself. Hearts are trump. Your opponent leads K♠︎. You hold A♠︎ and 7♥︎. Which may you play?

Only A♠︎. You have a spade, so you must follow suit. You could only trump with 7♥︎ if you had no spades.

4Double tricks and the hidden second card

The first 6 tricks are double tricks. Each player plays two cards, in three moves.

  1. The leader plays a card face up.
  2. The other player plays a card face up.
  3. Both players put a second card face down at the same time, then turn them up.

The second cards must follow the led suit too, even the leader’s own. Then the highest of all four cards wins the trick, by the same rule as before.

B leadsK♠︎
A follows7♠︎
B’s second cardA♠︎
A’s second card7♥︎

Hearts are trump. B still has spades, so B’s second card must be a spade, and B puts down the Ace. A has no spades left and puts down 7♥︎. Both turn them up, and A’s trump wins all four cards.

Since all 32 cards are in play, you often know which cards your opponent holds. The second card is where you bluff and guess.

Check yourself. Clubs are led in a double trick, and you still hold one club. Must your second card be that club?

Yes, if you didn’t already play it as your first card. Every card in the trick must follow the led suit if you can.

5The lay-off, and the last four tricks

Before the first trick, both players choose 3 cards from their hand and set them aside face down at the same time. You keep 13 cards in hand, and you may look at your own 3 at any time.

The 6 double tricks use 12 of your cards. Then come 4 single tricks, with one card each.

  • In trick 7, both play their last card in hand.
  • Then both take their 3 set-aside cards into their hand for tricks 8 to 10.

The winner of the last trick, trick 10, scores 2 card points for it.

The word The 3 cards set aside are the lay-off.

Check yourself. How many cards do you play in a deal?

2 in each of 6 double tricks and 1 in each of 4 single tricks, so 16. That’s your whole hand, including the lay-off.

Try it now. Play one practice deal with steps 0 to 5. Deal 16 cards to each player. The non-dealer names a trump suit and leads first. Both set aside a lay-off, then play the 10 tricks and count your card points.

Part 2 The contract, the simple way

6Becoming the Declarer

Deal 10 cards to each player. Put 4 cards in the middle, one face up and three face down. The last 8 cards stay in a pile.

In the first two deals of your first match, skip the auction. Deal the 8 cards from the pile right away, 4 to each player, so you both hold 14. Then the non-dealer either takes the contract or passes. If they pass, the dealer may take it. If both pass, the same dealer deals again, and this doesn’t count as a deal.

Whoever takes the contract plays against the other. They play Simple, the easiest target (step 8). Before step 7, they may make it harder by adding their own Schneider token.

The words The player who takes the contract is the Declarer. The other is the Defender. The 4 cards in the middle are the talon, and the 8 are the stock.

7The talon, and the game type

The Declarer decides who gets the face-up talon card. Then both players take face-down talon cards until each holds 16 cards. Nobody looks at a face-down talon card before taking it.

Now the Declarer names the game type.

A trump game
The Declarer names a suit as trump.
No-trump
There is no trump suit.
Nullo
No-trump with a reversed goal (step 10). Leave it out in the first three deals.

Then both players set aside their lay-off, and the Defender leads the first trick.

The word This way of sharing the talon is First Pick.

Check yourself. In one of your first two deals, both players hold 14 cards. The Declarer gives the face-up talon card to the Defender. How many face-down talon cards does each player take?

The Defender takes 1 and the Declarer takes 2. The face-up card brings the Defender to 15, so they need one more for 16. The Declarer needs two.

8The target

The Declarer wants the Defender to win as few card points as possible. The target says how many the Defender may win at most.

TargetLevelsTrump gameNo-trump
Simple01411
Schneider497
Schwarz843

If the Defender wins no more than that, the contract is made. If they win even one card point more, it fails. The 2 points for the last trick count too. Harder targets are worth more levels. Leave Schwarz out in the first three deals.

Check yourself. A trump game with Simple. The Defender wins 15 card points. Is the contract made?

No. With Simple in a trump game, the Defender may win at most 14. With 15, the contract fails.

9Scoring

At the end of a deal, score in this order.

  1. Card points. Both players score the card points they won.
  2. The contract. If it is made, the Declarer scores 12 + 3 per level. If it fails, the Defender scores 12 + 6 per level, so the levels count double.
LevelsMade, Declarer scoresFailed, Defender scores

If the Declarer doesn’t follow suit although they could, the contract fails. If the Defender does it, the contract is made.

Check yourself. Hearts are trump, and the Declarer plays Simple. The Defender wins 12 card points. What does each player score?

The contract is made, since 12 is no more than 14. The Defender scores 12. The Declarer scores the other 33 − 12 = 21 card points, plus 12 for the contract, so 33.

Try it now. Play deals 1 and 2 of your first match this way. A match is 6 deals, and you take turns dealing.

Part 3 Nullo, handicaps and the auction

10Nullo

Nullo is played like No-trump, but the Declarer wants to win as few card points as possible themselves. So the target limits the Declarer, with the same numbers as in No-trump (11, 7 or 3).

When scoring card points in Nullo, each player scores the card points in their opponent’s pile.

Check yourself. In Nullo with Simple, the Declarer wins 9 card points and the Defender 17. Is the contract made, and who scores which card points?

Made, since 9 is no more than 11. The Declarer scores 17 card points, from the Defender’s pile, and the Defender scores 9.

11Handicaps

Besides a harder target, the Declarer can make the contract worth more by giving the Defender handicaps. Each handicap is a token worth some levels.

Call the Shots, 1 level
Before trick 7, and again before trick 8, the Defender decides who leads.
Second Pick, 1 level
The Declarer names the game type before the talon. Then the Defender decides who gets the face-up talon card, and both take face-down talon cards until each holds 16.
Double Pick, 2 levels
The Declarer names the game type before the talon. Then the Defender turns up one face-down talon card and picks any two of the four. The Declarer gets the other two.
Sorry Not Sorry, 1 level, once
Once in the deal, the Defender may break suit although they could follow, with any card but the first of a trick. They say so when the card is face up. That card can’t win the trick, not even as a trump.
Fair Share, 2 levels, once
Once in the deal, when all cards of a trick are face up and before it is taken, each player puts their own cards from it on their own pile. The winner still leads next, and still scores 2 if it is the last trick.
Most Wanted, 2 levels, once
Once in the deal, before the Declarer plays any card but the first of a trick, the Defender names a card. If the Declarer holds it and may play it now, they must play it face up. As a second card, it comes before the Defender’s. Otherwise nothing happens, and the power is used up.

The three marked “once” are powers. The Defender chooses the trick, uses the power, and turns the token over.

The contract’s total is the levels of its target plus its handicaps, from 0 to 16. Only the target decides whether it is made.

Check yourself. A contract with Schneider, Second Pick and Fair Share. What is its total, and what is it worth if made?

4 + 1 + 2 = 7 levels. Made, it is worth 12 + 3 × 7 = 33. Failed, the Defender would score 12 + 6 × 7 = 54.

12Offers while the cards arrive

From deal 3 on, deal only 10 cards to each player at first. The 6 handicap tokens lie face down in a row. Your offer is the tokens in front of you, and its total is the levels you can see. With no tokens, you offer Simple, for a total of 0.

At 10 cards and again at 12 cards, play an offer round.

  1. The dealer turns up the next two tokens of the row. Face-up tokens stay there until someone takes them.
  2. The player with the lower total acts first, or the non-dealer if the totals are tied.
  3. Each player gets one turn. Add one token to your offer, or skip. It can be a face-up handicap token from the row or one of your own target tokens.
  4. The player with the higher total scores an offer bonus of 3. If the totals are tied, nobody does.
  5. The dealer deals 2 more cards to each player from the stock.

Your offer can only grow, and every token you add must raise your total.

If you add Schwarz while you have Schneider, lay it over Schneider, and lay Double Pick over Second Pick the same way. The covered token no longer counts. You can’t add Schneider once you have Schwarz, or Second Pick once you have Double Pick.

Check yourself. An offer round starts. Your total is 2, and your opponent’s is 1. Who acts first? And if neither of you adds a token, who scores the offer bonus?

Your opponent acts first, because their total is lower. If nobody adds a token, your total is still higher, so you score the offer bonus of 3.

13The final round

At 14 cards, the dealer turns up the last two tokens, and the final round begins. The player with the lower total acts first, or the non-dealer if tied. Then you take turns.

  • Raise by adding tokens one at a time until your total is higher than your opponent’s. Stop as soon as it is.
  • Or pass. If you can’t get higher, you must pass.

A pass ends the auction. The other player becomes the Declarer, and their offer is the contract. They may still add tokens. Then they give their handicap tokens to the Defender, who uses them against the Declarer. All other tokens are set aside for this deal.

If both totals are 0, the player who would become the Declarer may decline. Then the same dealer deals again, and this doesn’t count as a deal.

Check yourself. In the final round, your total is 3 and your opponent’s is 5. You add Schneider. What happens next?

Your total is now 7, higher than 5, so you stop. Now your opponent must raise above 7 or pass. If they pass, you are the Declarer with a total of 7.

14Your first match, and every match after

Slamlin’s rules suggest learning it over your first match.

  • Deals 1 and 2 have no auction and no handicaps, as in Part 2.
  • Deal 3 is your first auction. Don’t shuffle the handicap tokens. The dealer turns up Call the Shots and Second Pick at 10 cards, Sorry Not Sorry and Fair Share at 12, and Most Wanted and Double Pick at 14.
  • From deal 4 on, play the full game, with shuffled tokens, Nullo and Schwarz.

A match is 6 deals, and you take turns dealing. After six deals, the higher score wins. If the scores are tied, the match is a draw.

★An auction and its result

10 cards

A deals. Fair Share and Call the Shots are turned up. B acts first and takes Call the Shots, a total of 1. A takes Fair Share, a total of 2. A is higher and scores the offer bonus of 3.

12 cards

Second Pick and Most Wanted are turned up. B is lower, acts first and takes Most Wanted, for 3. A takes Second Pick, also 3. The totals are tied, so nobody scores.

14 cards, the final round

Sorry Not Sorry and Double Pick are turned up. The totals are tied, so B acts first as the non-dealer and adds Schneider, for 7. A passes.

The contract

B is the Declarer with Schneider, Call the Shots and Most Wanted, a total of 7. B gives Call the Shots and Most Wanted to A. A’s own tokens are set aside. There is no talon handicap, so B shares the talon with First Pick and then names hearts as trump.

The result

With Schneider in a trump game, A may win at most 9 card points. A wins 8, so B makes the contract. A scores 8 card points, after the offer bonus of 3 earlier. B scores the other 25, plus 12 + 3 × 7 = 33 for the contract, so 58.

Part 4 More details

Small rules

Who deals

Choose the first dealer at random. After that, take turns dealing, so each player deals three times in a match.

Keeping score with chips

Put the chips in the middle as a bank. Each player takes their points from it and keeps them in their own stack.

The board

The rules PDF has a board as a play aid. Coins on it mark the deal, the offer totals, the game type and the stage points. You don’t need it to play.

Counting quickly

Count only the smaller pile, plus 2 if that player won the last trick. Subtract it from 33 in a trump game, or from 26 in No-trump and Nullo.

Other ways to play a match

The full rules have three optional variants. In Open Endgame, both lay-offs are shown after trick 7. The Stage Match and the Tour Match add a race of made contracts to the scoring. Try them once the standard match feels familiar.

Tips for your first matches

  • Plan your lay-off. Those 3 cards decide the last three tricks.
  • Count the cards. Since all 32 are in play, you can often work out exactly what your opponent still holds.
  • A failed contract scores double for your opponent. Offer only what your hand can carry.
  • When you are the Defender, save your powers for the trick where they hurt most.
