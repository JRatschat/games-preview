Concept

Knocklin is a Gin Rummy game for two with a standard deck. You draw and discard to form your cards into sets and runs. The cards outside your melds are your deadwood. When your deadwood is worth 10 or less, you may knock to end the deal. Your opponent may then risk a Long Shot to beat you.

Instead of scoring points, you move up through levels from Ace to King. One player is red and the other is black. Your wilds are the two cards of your level’s rank in your colour.

During the deal, you may show a meld by laying it face up in front of you. Your wilds only work in shown melds. A shown meld is fixed for you, but your opponent may lay off cards onto it. When you win a deal, you move up an extra level for each shown meld you have more than your opponent.

To win the match, you must move up past King in a deal where you have at least two shown melds.

Setup

Players: 2.

Cards: One standard deck of 52 cards, without Jokers.

Colours: One player is red (hearts and diamonds) and the other is black (spades and clubs) for the whole match. Choose at random.

Keeping score: Note each player’s colour and level on paper, or use a level stack. For a level stack, take a second deck with a different back. The red player takes the 13 hearts and the black player the 13 spades. Stack them face up from King at the bottom to Ace on top. The top card shows your level in your colour. When you move up, put cards from the top aside.

Levels: Both players start the match on Ace. For a short match, both start on level 5 instead.

Starting player: Choose the starting player for the first deal at random. After that, the loser of the last deal starts. The other player deals.

Deal: The dealer deals 10 cards to each player. The rest of the cards form the face-down stock. Turn the top card of the stock face up to start the discard pile.

A Deal at a Glance

  1. Deal: 10 cards each. The top card of the stock starts the discard pile.

  2. Turns: Draw a card. Then you may show one meld or lay off one card. Then discard.

  3. Knock: With 10 or less deadwood, discard face down and lay out your hand. With no deadwood, you have Gin and the deal ends. With no deadwood and no discard, you have Big Gin.

  4. Answer: The defender accepts the knock or takes a Long Shot.

  5. Compare: The defender may lay off. The lower deadwood wins, and a tie goes to the defender.

  6. Moving up: The winner of the deal moves up one or more levels. Shown melds cancel each other out, and each one the winner has more than the loser is worth 1 extra level.

Cards

Rank: Ace < 2 < 3 < … < 10 < Jack < Queen < King.

Value: Table 1 lists the value of each card. The value only matters for deadwood.

Levels and Wilds

Levels: The levels follow the rank, from Ace up to King.

Wilds: The two cards of your level’s rank in your colour are your wilds. For your opponent they are ordinary cards, also when both players are on the same level. Your wilds only work in melds you show (see Showing). Everywhere else, they count as themselves.

Example: You are the red player on level 7, so 7♥︎ and 7♦︎ are your wilds. If your opponent is also on level 7, their wilds are 7♠︎ and 7♣︎.

Melds and Deadwood

Set: 3 or 4 cards of the same rank, each of a different suit.

Run: 3 or more cards of the same suit in sequence, such as 4♠︎ 5♠︎ 6♠︎. A–2–3 is a run. Q–K–A is not.

One meld per card: Each card can be part of only one meld.

Lay off: To lay off, add one card from your hand to one of your opponent’s melds. The card must belong in the meld and not be in it yet as a natural card. If a wild stands for it, place it on the wild. A covered wild stays in the meld.

Deadwood: The cards in your hand that are not part of a meld are your deadwood. Your deadwood count is their total value.

Your Turn

First card: At the start of a deal, the starting player may take the face-up card as their draw. If they refuse it, the dealer may take it as their draw and play a full turn, and then the starting player takes their turn as usual. If both refuse it, the starting player draws from the stock.

Draw: Take the top card of the stock or the top card of the discard pile.

Show or lay off: You may then do one of the following:

  • Show one meld (see Showing).

  • Lay off one card onto one of your opponent’s shown melds.

Discard: Place one card from your hand face up on the discard pile. If you took the top discard this turn, you may not discard that same card, not even face down when you knock. Instead of discarding face up, you may knock (see Knocking).

Last card: After showing or laying off, you must still have at least one card you may discard. If you discard your last card, you must knock with it, and you have Gin (see Knocking). In a turn where you showed, you may not knock, so discard your last card face up and play on with no cards in hand. With no cards in hand, you draw from the stock.

Empty stock: If only 2 cards are left in the stock after a player discards face up, the deal ends without a winner. Nobody moves up, and the player who started this deal also starts the next one.

Showing

Show: Lay one meld from your hand face up in front of you. It is now a shown meld.

Wilds: In a shown meld, your wild stands for any one card that is not already in the meld, or it may be used as itself. In a run, place it where that card would go. In a set, it stands for any missing suit.

Natural cards: Every shown meld must contain at least two natural cards. A natural card is any card that is not used as a wild.

Fixed for you: You may not add cards to your shown melds, take cards from them or rearrange them. This also applies at the end of the deal.

Open for your opponent: Your opponent may lay off cards onto your shown melds.

Example: You are the red player on level 7 and show 5♠︎ 7♥︎ 7♠︎, with your wild 7♥︎ standing for 6♠︎. On their turn, your opponent lays off 4♠︎ onto it. Later you draw 6♠︎. You may not add it to your shown run, so it stays in your hand. Had your opponent drawn it, they could have laid it off onto your wild.

Example: You are the red player on level 7 and show 4♣︎ 4♦︎ 7♥︎ as a set of 4s. Your opponent may lay off 4♥︎ and 4♠︎ onto it. The first one covers your wild. After both, the set holds all four 4s and is full.

Knocking

Knock: If your deadwood count is 10 or less after your discard, you may knock. Place your discard face down instead of face up. You are now the knocker and the other player is the defender. Lay out your whole hand face up, divided into melds and deadwood. Melds you lay out when you knock are not shown melds, so your wilds count as themselves in them.

Not after showing: You may not knock in a turn where you showed a meld, not even with Gin or Big Gin. You may lay off and knock in the same turn.

Gin: If you knock with no deadwood at all, you have Gin. The deal ends at once and you win it. The defender does not answer and does not lay off.

Big Gin: If all the cards in your hand form melds before you discard, you may knock without discarding. This is Big Gin. It ends the deal like Gin, and you move up more levels.

Answer

If the knocker does not have Gin, the defender chooses one of these before laying out their hand:

  • Accept: Go straight to Compare.

  • Long Shot: Draw the top card of the stock and discard one card face up. Then go to Compare. If the knocker still wins, they move up 2 levels instead of 1.

Nothing else: During a Long Shot, the defender may not show or knock. They lay off afterwards, as usual in Compare. The knocker’s laid-out hand stays as it is. The empty stock rule does not apply after a knock.

Compare

Laying off: The defender lays out their hand in melds and deadwood. They may then lay off any number of cards onto the knocker’s melds, both shown and laid out. The knocker never lays off onto the defender’s melds.

Winner: If the knocker has less deadwood, the knocker wins the deal. If the defender has equal or less deadwood, this is an undercut and the defender wins the deal. A defender with no deadwood also has an undercut, because only the knocker can have Gin.

Moving Up

The winner of the deal moves up the number of levels in Table 2. Shown melds cancel each other out one for one, so the winner moves up 1 extra level for each shown meld they have more than the loser. The loser never moves.

Table 2. Level changes
Result Levels
Knocker wins Knocker +1
Knocker wins after a Long Shot Knocker +2
Undercut Defender +2
Gin Knocker +3
Big Gin Knocker +4
Per shown meld more than the loser Winner +1

Example: A is on level 7 and B is on level 5. Each has one shown meld. A knocks with 6 deadwood. B’s deadwood is a King, a 9 and a 3. B could lay off the 9 onto A’s set of 9s, but 13 deadwood would still lose, so B takes a Long Shot. B draws 4♥︎, which fits a run in their hand, and discards the King. After laying off the 9, B has 3 deadwood. This is an undercut. Their shown melds cancel each other out, so B moves up 2 levels, to level 7. A stays on level 7.

Match End

If you would move up past King, you win the match, but only if you have at least two shown melds of your own in that deal. Your opponent’s shown melds do not matter for this. Otherwise, you stop on King, and any extra levels are lost.

Example: A is on Queen, shows one run and has Gin. B has no shown meld, so A would move up 3 + 1 = 4 levels. With only one shown meld, A stops on King. In the next deal, A shows a run and a set and later knocks. B accepts, and A has less deadwood. A moves up past King and wins the match.

Design Notes and Credits

Knocklin is my own take on Gin Rummy. The private level wilds and the climb through the ranks are inspired by the Chinese card game Guan Dan.
