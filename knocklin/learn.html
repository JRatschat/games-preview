Learn Knocklin

Knocklin is a card game for two, built on Gin Rummy. You sort your cards into groups and race each other up a ladder from Ace to King. This page teaches it one idea at a time, as simply as possible.

The whole game in short

  1. You each hold 10 cards and sort them into neat groups. On every turn you take one card and give one away.
  2. You may lay a group face up on the table. Only there do your two wild cards work, and it can earn you extra levels.
  3. When the cards that don’t fit into a group are worth very little, you knock. That ends the deal, and whoever has less left over wins it.
  4. The winner moves up a ladder from Ace to King. Your wild cards change with every level. Moving up past King wins the match, if you showed two melds in that deal.

What you need, and how this page works

  • Two players.
  • One standard deck of 52 cards, without Jokers.
  • Pen and paper to note your levels, or a second deck (see Part 3).

Part 1 (steps 0 to 5) is how a deal is played. It’s mostly Gin Rummy, and after step 4 you play a practice deal. Part 2 (steps 6 to 10) adds the ladder and the wild cards, which is what makes Knocklin special. Part 3 holds smaller rules. They matter in play, but you don’t need them while learning. If you forget what a word means, Part 3 also has a list of words.

Already know Gin Rummy? Steps 1 to 4 are plain Gin Rummy, so start at step 5.

Part 1 Playing a deal

0The cards

A deck has 52 cards. Each card shows a symbol and a number or letter. There are four symbols in two colours.

  • ♥︎ Hearts, red
  • ♦︎ Diamonds, red
  • ♠︎ Spades, black
  • ♣︎ Clubs, black

The word The symbol is the card’s suit. The number or letter is its rank.

Each suit has 13 cards, in this order.

A2345678910JQK

A is the Ace, the lowest card. J is the Jack, Q the Queen and K the King, the highest card.

On this page, “7♥︎” means the 7 of hearts.

1Neat groups

Your goal is to sort your cards into neat groups. There are two kinds.

A set is cards of the same rank

3 or 4 cards of the same rank, like three 9s or four Kings, each of a different suit.

9♣︎9♦︎9♥︎

A run is cards in a row, in one suit

3 or more cards of the same suit, one after the other, like stairs.

4♠︎5♠︎6♠︎

A‑2‑3 is a run. Q‑K‑A is not, because the Ace is the lowest card and never comes after the King. Each card can be in only one group at a time.

The word A neat group is a meld. Sets and runs are melds.

A7♦︎7♥︎7♠︎
B5♦︎6♦︎7♣︎
CQ♠︎K♠︎A♠︎
D8♥︎9♥︎10♥︎J♥︎
Check yourself. Which of these are melds?

A is a set. B is not, because 7♣︎ is a different suit. C is not, because the Ace never comes after the King. D is a run of four.

3Taking a turn

Getting ready

Shuffle and deal 10 cards to each player. Put the rest face down in the middle. That’s the stock. Turn its top card face up next to it to start the discard pile.

Stock, face down
Discard pile, face up8♦︎

Your turn has two parts

  1. Draw one card, either the top card of the stock (a surprise) or the top card of the discard pile (you can see it).
  2. Discard one card by putting it face up on the discard pile.

Now you have 10 cards again, and it’s the other player’s turn.

One small rule. If you drew the top card of the discard pile, you can’t discard that same card in the same turn.

For a practice deal, just pick who starts. The exact rule for the first turn is in Part 3.

4Knocking

Knocking ends the deal. You may knock when your deadwood is 10 or less after you discard.

How to knock

  1. Put your discard face down. That says “I knock”.
  2. Lay your whole hand face up on the table, sorted into melds and deadwood.

Here you draw 7♠︎, which fits your run. Then you discard K♦︎, and your deadwood count is 3 + 2 + 1 = 6.

Run4♠︎5♠︎6♠︎7♠︎
Set9♣︎9♦︎9♥︎
DeadwoodK♦︎3♥︎2♣︎A♠︎

6 is 10 or less, so you may knock.

The word The player who knocks is the knocker. The other player is the defender.

The defender gets a little help

The defender also lays out their hand, sorted into melds and deadwood. Then they may add their deadwood to the knocker’s melds, if it fits. A card fits if it makes a run longer or turns a set of 3 into a set of 4. The knocker can’t do this.

Knocker’s run4♠︎5♠︎6♠︎7♠︎
Defender adds 8♠︎8♠︎

The word Adding a card to the other player’s meld is laying off.

Who wins the deal

  • Both players add up their deadwood. Less deadwood wins.
  • A tie goes to the defender. Knocking says “I have less”, so if that’s not true, the knocker loses. This is an undercut.
  • If you knock with no deadwood at all, that’s Gin. You win at once, and the defender can’t lay off.
Check yourself. The knocker has 6 deadwood. The defender has 9, then lays off a 4. Who wins?

The defender now has 9 − 4 = 5, which is less than 6. It’s an undercut, and the defender wins.

Try it now. Play one practice deal with steps 1 to 4. Don’t worry about who wins.

5One last try with the Long Shot

When someone knocks without Gin, the defender chooses before laying out their hand.

  • Accept. Lay out your hand and compare, as in step 4.
  • Long Shot. First draw the top card of the stock and discard one card face up. Then lay out your hand and compare, as in step 4.

The Long Shot is a gamble. It can save a close deal, but if the knocker still wins, the knocker moves up more levels (step 6).

During a Long Shot, the defender can’t knock and can’t show a meld. Showing comes in step 8.

The knocker has 6 deadwood. You are the defender, and your deadwood is these three cards. The knocker has a set of 9s, so you could lay off your 9.

Your deadwoodK♥︎9♠︎3♣︎
Check yourself. Should you accept or take a Long Shot?

Take a Long Shot. If you accept, you lay off the 9 and still have 10 + 3 = 13, which loses. With a Long Shot, you might draw a card that fits a meld in your hand, so you can discard the King instead. Then you lay off the 9, have just 3 left, and win with an undercut.

Part 2 The ladder and the wild cards

6Moving up the ladder

Knocklin doesn’t add up points over many deals. Instead, both players start at the bottom of the ladder, on the Ace. When you win a deal, you move up. The loser stays where they are. Nobody ever moves down.

The word Your place on the ladder is your level. “I’m on level 7” means you’ve moved up to the 7.

This is how many levels the winner moves up.

How the deal endedWinner moves up
The knocker wins1 level
The knocker wins after a Long Shot2 levels
Undercut, the defender wins2 levels
Gin3 levels
Big Gin (rare, see Part 3)4 levels

Write down both levels after every deal. Step 9 adds a bonus on top.

7Your colour and your wild cards

At the start of the match, choose at random who is red (♥︎ ♦︎) and who is black (♠︎ ♣︎). You keep your colour for the whole match.

You have two wild cards. A wild card can stand in for a card you’re missing. Your two are the cards of your level’s rank in your colour.

Your levelRed player’s wildsBlack player’s wilds
AceA♥︎A♦︎A♠︎A♣︎
77♥︎7♦︎7♠︎7♣︎
JackJ♥︎J♦︎J♠︎J♣︎

The word Wild cards are called wilds.

  • Only you can use your wilds. For the other player they are ordinary cards.
  • They change when you move up. Red on level 2 has 2♥︎ and 2♦︎. On level 5 it’s 5♥︎ and 5♦︎.
  • Important. A wild only works in a meld you show, which is the next step. In your hand, it’s just an ordinary card.
Check yourself. You’re black and on level 4. What are your wilds?

4♠︎ and 4♣︎.

8Showing a meld

On your turn, after you draw and before you discard, you may lay one meld face up on the table in front of you. That’s showing it.

Draw 1→Maybe show 1 meld→Discard 1

The cards you show leave your hand, so they can never be deadwood.

Good

  • Your wilds work, but only here.
  • If you win the deal, you can move up extra levels (step 9).

Bad

  • The meld is fixed. You can never add to it, take from it or change it.
  • The other player may lay off onto it.
  • You can’t knock in the turn you show.

How a wild fits in

In a shown meld, a wild stands for any one card that’s missing. Or you use it as itself, like an ordinary card.

The word A card that isn’t used as a wild is a natural card. Every shown meld needs at least two natural cards.

In a run, put the wild where the missing card would go.

Red on level 7, 7♥︎ is the 10♠︎9♠︎7♥︎J♠︎

In a set, the wild stands for any missing suit.

Red on level 7, a set of 4s4♣︎4♦︎7♦︎

The other player could add both 4♥︎ and 4♠︎ here. The first one covers the wild. Then the set holds all four 4s and is full.

Using a wild as itself. Red on level 7 shows 7♥︎ 8♥︎ 7♦︎. Here 7♥︎ is just the 7♥︎, and 7♦︎ stands for the 9♥︎. So the meld has two natural cards, 7♥︎ and 8♥︎.

Red on level 77♥︎8♥︎7♦︎

5♣︎7♥︎7♦︎

Check yourself. You’re red on level 7. May you show 5♣︎ 7♥︎ 7♦︎ as a run, with your wilds as 6♣︎ and 7♣︎?

No. Both wilds stand for other cards, so 5♣︎ is the only natural card. A shown meld needs two.

8♠︎ and Q♠︎ at the ends, and 10♠︎ on top of your 7♥︎, which stands for it.

9The bonus for showing

At the end of the deal, count each player’s shown melds. Think of a tug of war. One shown meld from each player cancels out. The winner moves up 1 extra level for each shown meld they have more than the loser.

Winner showedLoser showedExtra levels
20+2
21+1
11+0
02+0

So showing pays most when you win. But even when you lose, your shown melds make the winner’s bonus smaller.

Check yourself. You knock and win. You showed 2 melds, the other player 1. How many levels do you move up?

You get 1 for winning as the knocker and 1 extra for one more shown meld. That makes 2 levels.

10Winning the match

Move up past King and you win the match, but only if you have shown at least two melds yourself in that deal. If you haven’t, you stop on King and try again next deal.

Check yourself. You’re on Queen. You win with Gin, but you showed only one meld. What happens?

You stop on King, because you showed only one meld. Next deal, show two melds and win it.

For a shorter match, both players start on level 5 instead of the Ace.

Part 3 More details

Small rules

Who starts, and the first face-up card

In the first deal, choose the starting player at random. After that, the loser of the last deal starts, and the other player deals.

The starting player may take the face-up card as their draw. If they don’t want it, the dealer may take it and play a full turn, and then the starting player takes their turn as usual. If nobody wants it, the starting player draws from the stock.

The stock runs low

If only 2 cards are left in the stock after someone discards face up, the deal ends without a winner. Nobody moves up, and the same player starts the next deal.

This doesn’t apply after a knock, so a Long Shot is always possible.

Your last card

After showing or laying off, you must still have a card you’re allowed to discard. If you discard your last card, you knock with it, and that’s Gin.

But in a turn where you showed, you can’t knock. Then discard your last card face up and play on with no cards. On your next turn, draw from the stock.

Big Gin

Sometimes all your cards fit into melds before you discard. Then you may knock without discarding. That’s Big Gin. It ends the deal like Gin, and you move up 4 levels.

Wilds when you knock

The melds you lay out when you knock are not shown melds, so your wilds count as ordinary cards there. If they don’t fit, they are deadwood.

Laying off and knocking

You may lay off one card onto a shown meld and then knock in the same turn. You can’t show and knock in the same turn.

A defender with no deadwood

Only the knocker can have Gin. A defender who ends with no deadwood wins with an undercut.

Keeping track with a second deck

Instead of paper, use a second deck with a different back. Red takes the 13 hearts and black the 13 spades. Stack them face up, King at the bottom and Ace on top. The top card shows your level. When you move up, put cards from the top aside.

Tips for your first matches

  • Discard Jacks, Queens and Kings early if they don’t fit. Each is 10 points of deadwood.
  • Watch what the other player takes from the discard pile, and don’t discard what they collect.
  • Knocking early is sometimes better than waiting for Gin. It depends!
  • Show when you expect to win the deal. A shown meld is fixed, and the other player can lay off onto it.
  • Take a Long Shot when you’re close to the knocker. If it fails, the knocker moves up 2 instead of 1.
  • Near the top of the ladder, remember that you need two shown melds to finish.
